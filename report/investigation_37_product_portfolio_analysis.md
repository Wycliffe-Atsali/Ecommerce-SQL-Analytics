# Investigation 37 – Product Portfolio Analysis

## Phase 8 – Strategic Business Analytics

---

# Business Context

In a competitive e-commerce marketplace, evaluating products solely by sales volume provides an incomplete picture of portfolio performance. While some products generate substantial revenue, they may also incur high logistics costs or receive poor customer reviews. Conversely, products with moderate sales may consistently achieve excellent customer satisfaction and operational efficiency.

To make informed merchandising and inventory decisions, businesses require a balanced evaluation framework that considers financial performance, customer experience, operational costs, and competitive positioning simultaneously.

This investigation develops a Product Portfolio Scorecard by integrating multiple Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) into a single standardized Product Performance Index. The resulting scorecard provides management with a comprehensive view of product performance and supports strategic decision-making across the product portfolio.

---

# Business Objective

The objectives of this investigation were to:

- Calculate revenue generated by each product.
- Measure product demand using units sold.
- Evaluate customer satisfaction through review scores.
- Assess freight efficiency using average shipping costs.
- Measure category performance by comparing products within their respective categories.
- Standardize all KPIs onto a common 1–100 scoring scale.
- Develop a weighted Product Performance Score.
- Classify products into meaningful business performance tiers.
- Produce a reusable analytical dataset suitable for executive dashboards.

---

# Dataset Used

The analysis utilised the following tables from the Olist PostgreSQL database:

- **order_items**
- **products**
- **order_reviews**

These tables provided financial, operational, and customer satisfaction metrics necessary for constructing the Product Portfolio Scorecard.

# Analytical Approach

The Product Portfolio Scorecard was developed using a structured multi-stage analytical framework.

## Stage 1 – Product KPI Calculation

Individual KPIs were calculated for every product, including:

- Total Revenue
- Units Sold
- Average Review Score
- Average Freight Cost

Each KPI measures a different aspect of product performance and cannot be directly compared due to differing measurement scales.

---

## Stage 2 – Category Performance Assessment

Rather than assigning every product within a category the same performance value, products were evaluated relative to their direct competitors.

Each product was ranked within its own product category based on total revenue using PostgreSQL's `NTILE(100)` window function.

This approach rewards products that outperform others within their category while preventing artificially high scores simply because a product belongs to a high-revenue category.

---

## Stage 3 – KPI Standardisation

Because each KPI uses different units of measurement, all metrics were converted to a common 1–100 performance scale.

Different standardisation approaches were applied depending on the KPI:

| KPI | Standardisation Method |
|------|------------------------|
| Revenue | NTILE(100) |
| Units Sold | NTILE(100) |
| Review Score | Percentage Conversion (`Review ÷ 5 × 100`) |
| Freight Cost | Reverse NTILE(100) Ranking |
| Category Performance | NTILE(100) Within Product Category |

This ensured that higher scores consistently represented stronger business performance across all metrics.

---

## Stage 4 – Weighted Product Performance Score

A weighted scorecard was developed to reflect the relative importance of each KPI.

| KPI | Weight |
|------|--------|
| Revenue | **35%** |
| Units Sold | **25%** |
| Customer Reviews | **20%** |
| Freight Efficiency | **10%** |
| Category Performance | **10%** |

The final Product Performance Score represents the weighted contribution of each KPI and provides a single overall measure of product success.

---

## Stage 5 – Product Classification

Products were classified into business performance tiers using their weighted scores.

| Performance Score | Classification |
|-------------------|----------------|
| 90–100 | Star Product |
| 75–89 | High Performer |
| 60–74 | Growth Product |
| 45–59 | Stable Product |
| Below 45 | Underperforming Product |

These classifications simplify interpretation for business stakeholders and support portfolio management decisions.

---

# Key Findings

The Product Portfolio Analysis revealed several important insights:

- Revenue alone is not sufficient to identify high-performing products.
- Some products achieved excellent customer satisfaction despite generating moderate sales.
- Freight efficiency significantly influenced overall product performance by highlighting operational cost differences.
- Category-relative ranking provided a fair comparison between products competing within the same market segment.
- The weighted scorecard successfully combined financial, operational, and customer-focused metrics into a unified performance index.

---

# Business Interpretation

The Product Portfolio Scorecard provides management with a balanced framework for evaluating product performance.

Rather than relying solely on financial metrics, the scorecard considers customer satisfaction, operational efficiency, and competitive positioning. This enables decision-makers to distinguish between products that merely generate revenue and those that deliver sustainable business value.

The inclusion of category-relative performance ensures that products are assessed against appropriate competitors, producing a fairer and more meaningful evaluation.

By combining multiple KPIs into a single Product Performance Score, management can quickly identify products requiring investment, optimisation, or potential discontinuation.

---

# Business Recommendations

Based on the Product Portfolio Analysis, the following recommendations are proposed:

### 1. Invest in Star Products

Increase inventory availability and marketing support for products classified as **Star Products**, as they consistently demonstrate strong financial and operational performance.

### 2. Improve High-Potential Products

Investigate Growth Products with strong customer reviews but lower sales volumes to identify opportunities for increased visibility and promotion.

### 3. Review Freight Efficiency

Analyse products with high freight costs to identify opportunities for packaging optimisation, supplier relocation, or logistics improvements.

### 4. Monitor Underperforming Products

Products classified as **Underperforming** should be reviewed for pricing, customer satisfaction issues, product quality, or inventory strategy before considering removal from the catalogue.

### 5. Incorporate Scorecard into Executive Reporting

Integrate the Product Performance Score into Business Intelligence dashboards to enable continuous monitoring of portfolio performance.

# Limitations

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting the results:

- Product profitability could not be measured because product cost information was unavailable.
- Marketing expenditure and promotional campaigns were not included.
- Product return rates were unavailable.
- Inventory levels and stock availability were not considered.
- Customer lifetime contribution by product could not be evaluated.
