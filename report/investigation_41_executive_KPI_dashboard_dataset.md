# Investigation 41 — Executive KPI Dashboard Dataset

## Executive KPI Dashboard Dataset

---

## 1. Business Context

As the Olist e-commerce business grows, senior management requires a concise and reliable view of overall business performance.

Throughout the earlier investigations, individual aspects of the business have been analysed separately, including:

* Customer behaviour
* Customer Lifetime Value
* Repeat purchasing
* Seller performance
* Product performance
* Revenue
* Delivery performance
* Customer satisfaction
* Monthly sales trends

Although these investigations provide valuable insights, executive decision-making requires these metrics to be brought together into a consistent KPI framework.

An executive dashboard should not require management to interpret multiple independent SQL queries. Instead, the underlying analytical dataset should provide a trusted and reusable source containing the most important business KPIs.

This investigation therefore focuses on designing a SQL dataset that could serve as the foundation for an executive Business Intelligence dashboard.

---

# 2. Business Objective

The objective of this investigation is to create a reliable executive KPI dataset that consolidates the company's most important performance indicators into a reusable SQL structure.

The analysis focuses on:

* Total Revenue
* Total Delivered Orders
* Active Customers
* Average Order Value
* Average Customer Lifetime Value
* Repeat Purchase Rate
* Average Delivery Time
* Average Review Score
* Monthly KPI performance
* Monthly Revenue Growth

The final output should be suitable for consumption by a Business Intelligence tool such as Power BI, Tableau, or another reporting platform.

---

# 3. Analytical Objectives

The investigation is designed to answer the following analytical questions.

### Q1 — What is total delivered revenue?

Determine the total revenue generated from delivered orders.

### Q2 — How many orders were delivered?

Measure delivered orders and compare them with the total order population.

### Q3 — How many active customers does the business have?

Measure customers associated with at least one delivered order.

### Q4 — What is the Average Order Value?

Determine the average revenue generated per delivered order.

### Q5 — What is the average Customer Lifetime Value?

Calculate the average lifetime revenue generated by customers with delivered orders.

The analysis also examines the CLV distribution through:

* 25th percentile
* Median
* 75th percentile

### Q6 — What percentage of customers are repeat purchasers?

Determine the proportion of customers who completed more than one delivered order.

### Q7 — How long does an order take to reach the customer?

Calculate overall delivery performance using:

* Average delivery time
* Median delivery time
* 75th percentile delivery time
* Minimum delivery time
* Maximum delivery time

### Q8 — How satisfied are customers?

Evaluate customer satisfaction using review scores.

The analysis examines:

* Average review score
* Median review score
* Review score distribution
* Percentage of reviews represented by each score

### Q9 — How is revenue changing over time?

Calculate monthly revenue and month-over-month revenue growth.

### Q10 — Which sellers generate the highest sales value?

Rank sellers according to delivered sales value.

An additional activity threshold is applied to distinguish established sellers from sellers with very limited order activity.

### Q11 — Are the major executive KPIs internally consistent?

Cross-check important metrics such as:

* Revenue
* Delivered orders
* Customers
* Average Order Value

This helps identify potential calculation inconsistencies before the metrics are used in a dashboard.

### Q12 — Can the executive KPIs be represented as a reusable KPI dataset?

Create a dataset with:

> One row per KPI

This structure makes the output suitable for dashboard consumption.

### Q13 — Can the KPIs also be represented at monthly grain?

Create a monthly KPI dataset containing:

* Revenue
* Delivered orders
* Active customers
* Average Order Value
* Average delivery time

### Q14 — Does each dashboard dataset have the intended grain?

Validate that:

* The executive KPI dataset contains one row per KPI.
* The monthly KPI dataset contains one row per month.

### Q15 — Can the final KPI dataset be exposed through a reusable SQL view?

Create the final:

`executive_kpi_dashboard`

view.

# 4. Analytical Approach

The investigation follows a layered SQL architecture rather than calculating all metrics directly from the raw tables.

The general structure is:

```text
Raw Tables
    ↓
Delivered Orders
    ↓
Order-Level Revenue
    ↓
Customer-Level Metrics
    ↓
KPI-Level Aggregations
    ↓
Executive KPI Dataset
    ↓
Reusable SQL View
    ↓
BI Dashboard
```

This approach is important because different KPIs operate at different analytical grains.

---

# 5. KPI Grain Design

A major focus of this investigation is understanding **grain**.

The final executive KPI dataset uses:

> **One row = one KPI**

For example:

| KPI                             | Value |
| ------------------------------- | ----: |
| Total Revenue                   |   ... |
| Total Delivered Orders          |   ... |
| Active Customers                |   ... |
| Average Order Value             |   ... |
| Average Customer Lifetime Value |   ... |
| Repeat Purchase Rate            |   ... |
| Average Delivery Time           |   ... |
| Average Review Score            |   ... |

This structure is intentionally different from a transaction-level dataset.

The monthly dataset uses:

> **One row = one month**

For example:

| Month   | Revenue | Delivered Orders | Active Customers | AOV | Avg Delivery |
| ------- | ------: | ---------------: | ---------------: | --: | -----------: |
| Month 1 |     ... |              ... |              ... | ... |          ... |
| Month 2 |     ... |              ... |              ... | ... |          ... |

Explicitly defining the grain helps prevent duplicate KPI calculations and accidental multiplication caused by joins.

# 6. Core SQL Architecture

## 6.1 Delivered Orders

The first CTE establishes the population used throughout the dashboard.

Only orders with:

```sql
order_status = 'delivered'
```

are included.

This provides a consistent analytical population across revenue, customer, delivery, and satisfaction KPIs.

---

## 6.2 Order-Level Revenue

Revenue is first aggregated to:

> One row = one order

This is important because `order_payments` can contain multiple payment records for a single order.

The aggregation therefore calculates:

```text
Order Revenue = SUM(payment_value)
```

before calculating the Average Order Value.

This prevents payment-level rows from distorting order-level metrics.

---

## 6.3 Customer-Level Revenue

Customer revenue is then aggregated using:

```text
customer_unique_id
```

rather than `customer_id`.

This follows the customer identity logic established earlier in the project.

The resulting dataset has:

> One row = one customer

and contains the customer's lifetime delivered-order revenue.

---

## 6.4 Customer Order Frequency

A separate customer-level dataset calculates:

```text
Delivered Orders per Customer
```

This is then used to identify repeat customers.

A customer is classified as a repeat purchaser when:

```text
delivered_orders > 1
```

---

# 7. Executive KPI Definitions

## Total Revenue

Defined as:

```text
SUM(payment_value)
```

for delivered orders.

Revenue is calculated at order level before the overall total is produced.

---

## Total Delivered Orders

Defined as the number of distinct delivered orders.

This avoids counting multiple payment rows as separate orders.

---

## Active Customers

Defined as customers associated with at least one delivered order.

Customer uniqueness is based on:

```text
customer_unique_id
```

---

## Average Order Value

The investigation uses the order-level definition:

```text
Average Order Value =
Total Delivered Revenue
/
Total Delivered Orders
```

The calculation is therefore based on order-level revenue rather than raw payment rows.

---

## Average Customer Lifetime Value

Customer Lifetime Value is defined in this investigation as:

```text
Lifetime Revenue per Customer
```

where lifetime revenue represents the total delivered-order revenue associated with each unique customer.

The executive KPI then calculates:

```text
Average CLV =
Average Lifetime Revenue across Customers
```

---

## Repeat Purchase Rate

Repeat Purchase Rate is calculated as:

```text
Repeat Customers
/
Total Active Customers
× 100
```

A repeat customer is one with more than one delivered order.

---

## Average Delivery Time

Delivery time is calculated as:

```text
Delivered Customer Date
-
Order Purchase Timestamp
```

The result is converted into days.

---

## Average Review Score

Customer satisfaction is represented using the average review score from delivered orders that have reviews.

The review score scale ranges from 1 to 5.

---

# 8. Monthly KPI Analysis

The monthly dataset extends the executive KPI framework into a time-series structure.

Each month contains:

* Revenue
* Delivered orders
* Active customers
* Average Order Value
* Average delivery time

The month is derived from:

```sql
DATE_TRUNC('month', order_purchase_timestamp)
```

This creates a consistent monthly reporting period.

---

# 9. Monthly Revenue Growth

Monthly revenue is compared with the previous month using the `LAG()` window function.

The calculation is:

```text
Revenue Growth % =
(Current Month Revenue - Previous Month Revenue)
/
Previous Month Revenue
× 100
```

This allows management to distinguish between:

* Revenue expansion
* Revenue contraction
* Stable performance

The first month naturally has no previous month and therefore produces a NULL growth value.

---

# 10. Seller Analysis

The investigation also includes seller ranking as part of the executive KPI exploration.

Seller sales value is calculated using delivered order items.

The analysis provides:

* Seller
* Sales value
* Delivered orders
* Seller rank

An additional activity threshold is applied to identify sellers with sufficient order volume for more meaningful comparison.

The threshold used in the investigation is:

```text
10 delivered orders
```

This prevents very low-volume sellers from dominating an executive ranking simply because of a small number of transactions.

---

# 11. KPI Consistency Validation

Before creating the final dashboard view, the investigation performs a KPI consistency check.

The purpose is to ensure that key metrics are calculated from compatible populations and grains.

The validation compares:

* Total revenue
* Delivered orders
* Active customers
* Average Order Value

This is particularly important because the same business metric can produce different results if calculated at different grains.

For example:

```text
Payment-level revenue
```

should not be directly averaged to calculate AOV.

Instead:

```text
Payment Rows
      ↓
Order Revenue
      ↓
Average Order Value
```

This provides a more reliable analytical structure.

---

# 12. Grain Validation

Two explicit grain checks are included.

## Executive KPI Dataset

Expected grain:

```text
One row per KPI
```

The validation compares:

```sql
COUNT(*)
```

with:

```sql
COUNT(DISTINCT kpi)
```

If both values are equal, the dataset contains exactly one row per KPI.

---

## Monthly KPI Dataset

Expected grain:

```text
One row per month
```

The validation compares:

```sql
COUNT(*)
```

with:

```sql
COUNT(DISTINCT month)
```

This ensures that monthly aggregation has not introduced duplicate months.

# 14. Why a View Is Appropriate Here

Earlier investigations introduced CTEs as an important analytical technique.

In this investigation, CTEs remain the primary method for structuring the SQL logic.

However, the final result is exposed through a view because this investigation has moved from:

> "How do I calculate this metric?"

toward:

> "How do I provide a reusable dataset for reporting?"

The distinction is important.

### CTEs

Used for:

* Structuring complex SQL
* Breaking calculations into logical stages
* Controlling analytical grain
* Improving readability

### View

Used for:

* Reusing the final KPI dataset
* Providing a stable reporting interface
* Connecting SQL outputs to BI tools
* Avoiding repeated execution of the full query manually

Therefore, the investigation demonstrates both techniques rather than choosing one instead of the other.

# 16. Business Interpretation

The executive KPI dataset provides management with a consolidated view of the business across four major dimensions.

## Financial Performance

Revenue and Average Order Value provide a high-level indication of commercial performance.

Revenue growth provides additional context by showing whether performance is improving or declining over time.

---

## Customer Performance

Active customers, Customer Lifetime Value, and Repeat Purchase Rate provide insight into the strength of the customer base.

A high number of active customers does not necessarily imply strong customer retention.

Repeat Purchase Rate and CLV provide additional evidence regarding customer value and loyalty.

---

## Operational Performance

Average Delivery Time provides an overall measure of fulfilment efficiency.

However, the average should not be interpreted in isolation.

The median and 75th percentile provide additional information about the distribution of delivery times and can reveal whether a small number of very long deliveries are influencing the average.

---

## Customer Satisfaction

Review scores provide a customer-facing measure of business performance.

Combining satisfaction with operational metrics is particularly valuable because poor delivery performance may contribute to lower customer satisfaction.

# 17. Business Recommendations

The final recommendations should be based on the actual SQL results rather than assumptions.

Potential recommendation areas include:

### 1. Strengthen Customer Retention

If Repeat Purchase Rate is relatively low, management should investigate:

* Customer re-engagement campaigns
* Loyalty programmes
* Personalised offers
* Post-purchase engagement

---

### 2. Improve High-Value Customer Retention

If CLV is concentrated among a relatively small group of customers, the business should consider prioritising these customers for retention initiatives.

---

### 3. Monitor Delivery Performance

If average or upper-percentile delivery times are high, operational teams should investigate:

* Seller performance
* Geographic constraints
* Product categories
* Freight processes

---

### 4. Monitor Customer Satisfaction

If review scores show meaningful concentrations of low ratings, the business should investigate the underlying drivers.

Delivery performance, seller quality, product quality, and customer service should be considered together.

---

### 5. Investigate Revenue Growth Trends

Periods of negative month-over-month growth should be investigated to determine whether they are associated with:

* Reduced customer activity
* Lower order volume
* Lower Average Order Value
* Seasonal effects
* Seller or category performance

# 18. Limitations

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting the dashboard.

## 18.1 Delivered Orders Only

Most KPIs use delivered orders as the analytical population.

This provides consistency but means cancelled, unavailable, or otherwise non-delivered orders are excluded from the core KPI calculations.

---

## 18.2 CLV Definition

Customer Lifetime Value is defined here as historical delivered revenue.

It does not represent a predictive CLV model.

A more advanced analysis could incorporate:

* Customer retention probability
* Expected future purchases
* Customer lifespan
* Profit margins

---

## 18.3 Revenue Definition

Revenue is based on `payment_value`.

This represents transaction value rather than accounting profit.

Therefore, the KPI should not be interpreted as net profit.

---

## 18.4 Delivery Time

Delivery time measures the elapsed period between purchase and customer delivery.

It does not directly measure:

* Warehouse processing time
* Seller handling time
* Carrier transit time

These could be investigated separately.

---

## 18.5 Review Coverage

Not every delivered order necessarily has an available review.

Therefore, customer satisfaction metrics represent the reviewed population rather than every delivered order.
