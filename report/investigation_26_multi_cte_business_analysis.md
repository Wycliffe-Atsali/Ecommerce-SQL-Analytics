# Investigation 26 – Multi-CTE Business Analysis

## Business Objective

The objective of this investigation was to learn how multiple Common Table Expressions (CTEs) can be combined to solve increasingly complex business problems while maintaining readable, modular, and maintainable SQL code.

Unlike previous investigations that relied on a single CTE, this investigation focused on constructing analytical pipelines where the output of one CTE becomes the input for the next. This mirrors how SQL is commonly written in professional Business Intelligence and Data Analytics environments.

---

# Business Context

Business questions often require combining several intermediate calculations before arriving at a final answer.

For example, answering the question:

> *Which customer segment contributes the most revenue?*

cannot be answered directly from a single table.

Instead, the analysis must first determine:

1. The number of orders per customer.
2. The total revenue generated by each customer.
3. A combined customer summary.
4. A customer classification based on purchasing behaviour.
5. Revenue generated by each customer segment.

Breaking these calculations into multiple CTEs makes the analytical process easier to understand, validate, and maintain.

---

# Database Thinking

This investigation required understanding how data flows across several related tables.

### Customer Analysis

```
customers
      │
      │ customer_id
      ▼
orders
      │
      │ order_id
      ▼
order_payments
```

Customer information was joined with orders to determine purchasing behaviour, while payment information was incorporated to calculate customer revenue.

---

### Seller Analysis

```
sellers
     │
     │ seller_id
     ▼
order_items
     │
     │ order_id
     ▼
orders
```

Seller performance was evaluated using order fulfilment records together with product revenue.

---

# Methodology

The investigation followed a layered analytical approach.

### Step 1

Build a reusable CTE that calculates customer order frequency.

↓

### Step 2

Create another reusable CTE that calculates customer revenue.

↓

### Step 3

Merge both CTEs into a unified customer summary.

↓

### Step 4

Create a third CTE to classify customers into business segments.

↓

### Step 5

Use the segmented data to answer business questions regarding customer distribution, revenue contribution, and average spending.

↓

### Step 6

Repeat the modular approach for seller performance analysis.

# Business Questions Answered

The investigation answered the following questions:

1. How many orders has each customer placed?
2. How much revenue has each customer generated?
3. Can customer order behaviour and revenue be combined into one reusable dataset?
4. Who are the highest-value customers?
5. What are the average customer revenue and order counts?
6. How can customers be segmented using purchasing behaviour?
7. How many customers belong to each segment?
8. Which customer segment generates the most revenue?
9. Which customer segment has the highest average customer revenue?
10. Can seller performance be summarised using reusable CTEs?
11. Which sellers generate the highest revenue?
12. What are the average, highest, and lowest seller revenues?
13. What business insights can be drawn from customer segmentation?
14. What advantages do multiple CTEs provide over one large SQL query?
15. How can businesses benefit from modular SQL design?

# SQL Techniques Demonstrated

This investigation introduced several important SQL concepts.

### Multiple Common Table Expressions (CTEs)

Several CTEs were chained together to create a complete analytical workflow.

### Sequential CTE References

Each CTE built upon the results of previous CTEs.

### Multi-stage Business Analysis

Large analytical problems were decomposed into smaller, reusable transformations.

### Aggregate Functions

The investigation applied:

- `COUNT()`
- `SUM()`
- `AVG()`
- `MAX()`
- `MIN()`
- `ROUND()`

### CASE Expressions

Customer purchasing behaviour was translated into business-friendly customer segments.

### Multi-table JOINs

Customer and seller analyses required combining multiple related tables into unified analytical datasets.

---

# Key Findings

## Customer Segmentation

Customers were successfully categorised into:

- One-Time Customers
- Repeat Customers
- Loyal Customers

This created a reusable framework for analysing customer behaviour throughout the remainder of the project.

---

## Revenue Distribution

One-Time Customers generated the largest share of total revenue.

Although this initially appears positive, it also indicates that relatively few customers are returning to make additional purchases.

---

## Seller Performance

Seller summaries provided:

- Total revenue
- Total fulfilled orders
- Marketplace averages
- Highest-performing sellers

This demonstrated how reusable analytical datasets can support multiple business questions.

---

## Modular Query Design

Rather than creating one large SQL statement, the analysis was divided into logical stages.

This approach greatly improved readability and reduced the complexity of debugging.

---

# Business Interpretation

The findings suggest that the marketplace performs well at acquiring new customers but is less successful at retaining them.

While one-time customers currently account for the largest proportion of revenue, sustainable long-term growth depends on increasing the proportion of repeat and loyal customers.

The seller analysis also highlights opportunities to benchmark high-performing sellers and share best practices across the marketplace.

---

# Business Recommendations

Based on this investigation, several recommendations can be made.

### Improve Customer Retention

Introduce personalised follow-up campaigns after a customer's first purchase to encourage additional orders.

### Develop Loyalty Programmes

Reward customers who place multiple orders with discounts, exclusive offers, or membership benefits to increase customer lifetime value.

### Benchmark High-Performing Sellers

Analyse the operational practices of top-performing sellers and promote these best practices throughout the seller network.

### Standardise Modular SQL Development

Encourage analysts to structure complex SQL using multiple CTEs rather than large monolithic queries. This improves maintainability and collaboration across analytics teams.

# Challenges Encountered

Several challenges arose during this investigation.

- Learning how multiple CTEs reference one another.
- Understanding how to build SQL in sequential analytical layers.
- Avoiding repeated business logic while maintaining reusable intermediate datasets.
- Recognising where each transformation should occur within the analytical pipeline.

Working through these challenges reinforced the importance of planning SQL before writing it.

The investigation also showed that readability is a key aspect of high-quality SQL. Structuring queries into logical stages not only simplifies debugging but also makes collaboration with other analysts more effective.
