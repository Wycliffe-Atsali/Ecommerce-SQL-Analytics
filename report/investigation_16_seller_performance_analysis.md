# Investigation 16: Seller Performance Analysis

## Business Objective

The objective of this investigation was to evaluate seller performance across the Olist marketplace by analysing order fulfilment, product diversity, revenue generation, geographical distribution, and customer satisfaction. The analysis aims to identify high-performing sellers, understand regional seller performance, and provide business recommendations that support marketplace growth and customer satisfaction.

---

# Business Context

Olist operates as a marketplace that connects customers with independent sellers. Since seller performance directly affects customer experience and company revenue, it is important to continuously monitor operational efficiency, sales performance, and customer satisfaction.

This investigation extends the relational analysis introduced in the previous investigations by incorporating seller information with order, product, and review data. Rather than analysing transactions in isolation, the investigation evaluates seller performance using multiple business metrics.

# Key Findings

The investigation revealed substantial variation in seller performance across the marketplace.

Key observations include:

- A relatively small number of sellers fulfilled a disproportionately high number of orders.
- Seller product catalogues varied considerably, with some sellers specialising in only a few products while others maintained extensive inventories.
- Revenue generation was similarly concentrated among a limited group of high-performing sellers.
- Seller distribution differed across Brazilian states, indicating regional differences in marketplace participation.
- Customer satisfaction generally remained high among many of the highest-performing sellers, suggesting that operational success and customer experience are not mutually exclusive.

---

# Business Interpretation

Seller performance should not be evaluated solely on revenue generation.

High-performing marketplace partners demonstrate strength across multiple dimensions, including:

- Operational efficiency.
- Revenue contribution.
- Product diversity.
- Customer satisfaction.

Evaluating sellers using multiple performance indicators provides a more balanced understanding of marketplace quality than relying on financial metrics alone.

The investigation also reinforced the importance of selecting measures at the correct level of granularity. Revenue calculations were based on `order_items.price`, ensuring that each product contributed only its own value and preventing duplicate revenue calculations.

# Recommendations

Based on the analysis, the following recommendations are proposed:

- Develop long-term strategic partnerships with sellers who consistently generate high revenue while maintaining excellent customer review scores.
- Introduce seller incentive programmes that reward both commercial performance and customer satisfaction.
- Provide additional operational support and training for sellers demonstrating lower performance.
- Expand successful seller practices across the marketplace through knowledge sharing and best-practice initiatives.
- Develop executive dashboards that monitor seller performance using operational, financial, and customer experience metrics simultaneously.

# Challenges Encountered

Several analytical challenges were encountered during this investigation.

## Multi-table Relationships

Seller information, orders, products, and customer reviews are distributed across multiple normalized tables, requiring careful navigation of foreign key relationships.

## Data Granularity

Revenue analysis required an understanding of data grain.

Although both `order_items` and `order_payments` contain financial information, only `order_items.price` accurately represents revenue at the individual product level.

Using order-level payment values during product-level analysis would have resulted in duplicate revenue calculations.

## Review Attribution

Customer reviews are recorded at the order level rather than the seller level.

Consequently, linking reviews to sellers requires joining through `order_items` and `orders`.

This relationship introduces potential duplication when multiple products from the same seller exist within a single order. This limitation has been documented and will be revisited in later project phases using more advanced SQL techniques.
