# Investigation 14: Customer Purchasing Behaviour Analysis

## Business Objective

The objective of this investigation was to analyse customer purchasing behaviour by integrating customer, order, and payment data. The analysis aimed to understand purchasing frequency, customer spending patterns, geographic purchasing behaviour, and customer value. These insights can support customer segmentation, retention strategies, targeted marketing campaigns, and broader business decision-making.

---

# Business Context

In a normalized relational database, customer information, purchase records, and payment transactions are stored in separate tables. Analysing customer behaviour therefore requires combining multiple related tables using SQL JOINs.

Unlike previous investigations that focused on individual tables, this investigation introduced relational analysis by connecting customer, order, and payment data to answer business questions that cannot be solved from a single table.

---

# Learning Objectives

By completing this investigation, I was able to:

- Apply `INNER JOIN` to combine normalized tables.
- Navigate one-to-many relationships.
- Perform multi-table relational analysis.
- Aggregate customer purchasing metrics across several tables.
- Differentiate between relational identifiers and business identifiers.
- Interpret customer purchasing behaviour using SQL.

# Business Questions

## Section A – Purchase Frequency

1. How many orders has each unique customer placed?
2. Which customers have placed the highest number of orders?
3. How many unique customers have placed orders?
4. How many orders has each customer placed in each state?
5. Which states have the highest number of orders?

---

7. Which customers generated the highest revenue?
8. What is the average spending per customer?
9. What is the average order value for each customer?

12. Which cities have the highest average customer spending?

## Section D – Customer Value

# Key Findings

The investigation demonstrated that customer purchasing behaviour is highly uneven across the platform. While most customers make relatively few purchases, a smaller group contributes significantly more orders and revenue.

Customer-level analysis highlighted the importance of using `customer_unique_id` rather than `customer_id`, since the latter represents transactional records rather than the true business customer.

Revenue analysis also revealed that payment information must be aggregated carefully because a single order may contain multiple payment records. Incorrect aggregation can easily produce misleading business metrics.

Geographic analysis showed that purchasing activity and revenue vary considerably across Brazilian states and cities, providing opportunities for regional business strategies.

---

# Challenges Encountered

Several analytical challenges emerged during this investigation.

- Understanding how normalized tables are connected through primary and foreign keys.
- Identifying the appropriate business identifier (`customer_unique_id`) for customer-level analysis.
- Recognising the impact of one-to-many relationships on aggregate calculations.
- Distinguishing between similar business metrics such as total revenue, average spending, and average order value.
- Determining when `COUNT(DISTINCT ...)` is necessary to avoid duplicate counting.

These challenges reinforced the importance of understanding the database schema before writing SQL.

---

# Analyst Reflection

This investigation marked a significant transition from querying individual tables to analysing relationships across a normalized database.

Rather than beginning with SQL syntax, the investigation required identifying the business problem, determining which tables contained the required information, understanding how those tables were related, and then selecting the appropriate JOIN strategy.

The investigation also demonstrated that writing correct SQL is only one part of data analysis. Choosing the correct business identifier and appropriate aggregation method is equally important for producing meaningful business insights.

Overall, this investigation strengthened my understanding of relational database analysis and laid the foundation for more advanced SQL topics such as subqueries, Common Table Expressions (CTEs), and window functions.
